# How Long Would You Survive a Real 5-Star Wanted Level?

**Version 2 (9:23 as rendered).** Section timings below are from v1 planning; the rendered v2 runs 9:23. Segments marked [NEW in v2] were added to reach mid-roll length.

Format: What If / survival POV, long-form, target 10-11 min (about 1,600 words at 150 wpm).
Host: OZZIE, a night-shift owl in an oversized orange hi-vis vest who runs "the Odds Board", a split-flap survival counter. Ozzie narrates; "YOU" is a faceless paper-cutout everyperson (grey hoodie, no face, so it never needs lip sync).
Premise twist (keeps us away from glorifying crime): you did nothing wrong. A database error flags you as the most wanted person in the country. Running only makes it worse.
Setting: a sunny Florida beach city. Real places named only generically. No game footage, logos, characters, or lookalikes.

## 0:40 ONE STAR: "SOMEBODY NOTICED" (1:30)

OZZIE: In video games, one star means a cop saw you do something. In real life, one star is quieter, and much faster than you think.

OZZIE: Your car's plate goes onto what's called a hot list. That list gets pushed to license-plate cameras: small grey boxes on poles at intersections, highway ramps, and shopping-center exits. FACT: Tens of thousands of them across the US, and many cities share their reads with each other.

[VISUAL: map of the city. Small camera icons blink on, one by one, until the map is freckled with them.]

OZZIE: You pull out of the gas station and turn left. Camera. Merge onto the highway. Camera. Every one of them quietly says, "seen here, at this second."

OZZIE: Ninety seconds later, a patrol car three blocks away gets an alert on its laptop. The officer doesn't even need to see you. The map tells them where you're heading.

[Odds Board: STARS ★★☆☆☆]

OZZIE: Hint number one about this whole video: in real life, the stars don't go up because you do something dramatic. They go up because a system noticed you.

[NEW in v2] (visual: camera_map of phones and doorbells, Ozzie explains)

OZZIE: And the cameras on poles are only the ones the city owns. Look around that gas station. The clerk has a phone. The guy at pump four has a phone. The house across the street has a doorbell camera pointed right at the road. You were probably recorded a dozen times before you even started the engine. None of those people work for the police. But the moment your face is on the news, every one of them becomes a witness. In the old days, you had to worry about being seen. Today, you have to worry about being recorded... and then remembered.

## 2:10 TWO STARS: "THE CHASE" (1:45)

[SFX: siren whoop, distant.]

OZZIE: Now there's a cruiser in your mirror, lights on. And this is the moment that decides everything, because you have a car, and you have a choice.

OZZIE: Most people imagine a chase is about who's faster. It isn't. The police aren't trying to be faster than you. They're trying to be everywhere you are about to be.

[VISUAL: top-down city grid. Your car is a small orange arrow. Blue dots appear on side streets ahead of it, not behind it.]

OZZIE: While one car follows, a dispatcher talks to every other car in the area. They don't chase you. They drive to where you're going.

OZZIE: FACT: Many departments now call off risky high-speed chases on purpose, because chases are dangerous for everyone on the road. That sounds like good news for you. It isn't. When they stop chasing, it's usually because something better is already watching.

[SFX: distant rotor thump, getting louder.]

[Odds Board: STARS ★★★☆☆  SURVIVAL TIME 00:11:40]

[NEW in v2] (visual: stat card 120 MPH vs radio)

OZZIE: Here's a fun bit of physics. Your car, flat out, does maybe a hundred and twenty miles an hour, if it doesn't fall apart first. A police radio call travels at the speed of light. That's about six hundred and seventy million miles an hour. By the time you reach the next intersection, the description of your car is already there, waiting for you. You're not racing the officer behind you. You're racing the radio. And the radio has never lost.

[Odds Board: SURVIVAL TIME 09:20:00]

[NEW in v2] (visual: Ozzie explains night one; stat card 24 h awake = 0.10 BAC)

OZZIE: Now night falls on day one. Where do you sleep? A hotel wants an ID and a card. A friend's couch is the first place they'll check. So you sleep... somewhere. A bench. A bus shelter. A storm drain. You don't really sleep. Every engine sounds like a patrol car. And that matters more than you'd think. According to US workplace safety researchers, staying awake for twenty-four hours impairs you about as much as a blood alcohol level of zero point one zero. That's over the legal driving limit in every US state. So on day two, the most wanted person in the country is also, basically, drunk. Tired people make mistakes. They use the card. They call their mom. And that's exactly what the next level is waiting for.
