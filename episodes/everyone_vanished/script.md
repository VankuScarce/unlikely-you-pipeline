## WEEK 1 · EVERYTHING IS FREE, NOTHING WORKS

- `KEEP s30` Let's check on you. Because you have a problem nobody in history has ever had. Everything is free, and nothing works.
- `NEW n02` First thing to go: the internet. Cell towers run on backup batteries for a few hours, some on generators for a few days. Then your phone becomes a very nice flashlight with no one to call. *(FACT: tower backup times vary; hours to days is the usual range)*
- `KEEP s31` Water: towers push water down with gravity, pressure drops within days, fill every bathtub on day one.
- `KEEP s32` Fresh food rots within a week, but canned food lasts for years. Honey basically never goes off.
- `NEW n03` Small win, though. The supermarket freezers are thawing, so on day two you eat a whole tub of ice cream for breakfast. Nobody can stop you. It might be the best meal anyone eats for the next hundred years.
- `KEEP s33` What actually threatens you is the boring stuff. An infected cut. A bad tooth. There are no doctors anymore. There's you.
- `KEEP s34` So the smartest move of week one is raiding a pharmacy for antibiotics and a first-aid book. Then grabbing the sports car.

## MONTH 1 · THE COMFORTS GO ONE BY ONE

- `KEEP s37` The subway floods in about a day and a half without its pumps.
- `KEEP s38` One fire, nobody coming, a suburb burning roof to roof for days.
- `NEW n04` And at home, the comforts go one by one. Hot showers last only as long as there's pressure left in the gas pipes. After that, it's a bucket of water you warmed up on a camping stove. *(GUESS: how long gas pressure lasts; weeks is plausible, I don't state a number)*
- `NEW n05` Gas stations still have thousands of litres of fuel in their underground tanks, but the pumps need power. So you learn to siphon fuel with a hose. It tastes exactly as bad as you'd think.
- `NEW n06` But then you remember the house with the solar panels. You plug in a projector, put on a movie, and for two hours it's a normal Friday night. Scraps hates the explosions.

## MONTH 4 · THE QUIET (new section)

- `NEW n07` Here's the part survival guides never mention. Around month four, the danger fades, and something worse moves in. Boredom.
- `NEW n08` Every day is the same day. No news, no work, no group chat, nobody to tell when something funny happens. People who spend the long winter at Antarctic research stations describe the same thing: time blurs, sleep drifts, and small problems start to feel huge. *(FACT: "winter-over syndrome" is well documented)*
- `NEW n09` What gets them through is a routine and a reason to get up. So you build one. Wake up, feed Scraps, check the panels, read one book from the library. You start talking to him out loud. He's a good listener.
- `NEW n10` But a routine isn't a reason. And one night, on the porch, you finally ask the question you've been avoiding. What is all of this for?

## THE MISSION (new section)

- `NEW n11` Your answer surprises you. You're not going to just survive. You're going to try to bring people back.
- `NEW n12` You're one man. There's nobody left to start a family with. So you do what humans have always done when nature says no. You go and ask science.
- `NEW n13` The libraries are open, the university labs are empty, and you've got solar power and all the time in the world. And here's what you find.
- `NEW n14` In 2023, a team in Japan took skin cells from male mice, turned them into eggs, and got baby mice with two fathers. In 2025, scientists in China went further: mice with two dads that grew up into adults. Very few of them made it. But it worked. *(FACT: Hayashi lab, Nature 2023; Chinese Academy of Sciences, Cell Stem Cell, Jan 2025)*
- `NEW n15` Then there are the freezers. Fertility clinics store frozen embryos, possibly more than a million in the US alone. Each one is a tiny future person whose parents are gone. That's the variety one man could never give a new humanity on his own. *(FACT: estimates range up to about 1.5 million in the US; say "possibly more than a million")*
- `NEW n16` But they're kept in liquid nitrogen, and with nobody topping up the tanks, the nitrogen slowly boils away. That gives you a deadline. *(FACT: tanks need regular refills; exact time varies, so no number is stated)*
- `NEW n17` And the biggest problem of all: a baby needs a womb. In 2017, doctors in Philadelphia kept premature lambs growing for four weeks inside a fluid-filled plastic bag. In 2021, scientists in Israel grew mouse embryos outside the body for about six days. Nobody has ever grown a human that way. Not from start to finish. *(FACT: CHOP "biobag", Nature Communications 2017; Weizmann Institute, Nature 2021)*
- `NEW n18` So that's your mission. Make the eggs. Save the embryos. Build a womb. With a library card, a generator and a dog.

## ENDING (new, open ending)

- `NEW n19` Years pass. The roads disappear under the trees. The city goes quiet for good.
- `NEW n20` And on one hill, in a university building wrapped in vines, a single window is still lit. Inside, a machine hums. Something in it might be growing. Or maybe it isn't.
- `NEW n21` Did you do it? Could one person really restart the human race? That's the one question this world never got to answer.
- `NEW n22` So you tell me. Would you even try? Tell me in the comments. And if you want the version where the animals vanish too, that one's next.
